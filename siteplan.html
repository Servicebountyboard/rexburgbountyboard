01 / Overview

Real neighbors. Meaningful quests.

Purpose

Connect Rexburg residents who need a task completed with neighbors willing to help. Users create and verify accounts, post bounties, request claims, communicate, complete work, and earn points after poster approval.

Audience

Bounty posters need a clear way to describe work, choose a hunter, and confirm results. Bounty hunters need understandable opportunities, request status, task details, and completion feedback. One account can serve both roles.

Experience goals

  • Make it obvious who must act next and whether work is approved.
  • Keep materials, time, location, and contact rules easy to find.
  • Use the warmth of a tavern noticeboard without obscuring real service tasks.
  • Provide clear success, rejection, empty, loading, and error states.

Scope includes account verification, bounty creation and claiming, two approval stages, communication, notifications, optional ratings, and points. This plan does not claim those features are implemented.

02 / Branding

The tavern noticeboard

Combine the Oak & Ember noticeboard with Hearthside’s welcoming tone: parchment surfaces, walnut headings, burgundy seals, and restrained brass accents. The product name remains Rexburg Bounty Board.

Proposed wordmark and CSS monogram; no external image is required. Replace with a final team-approved logo later.

Visual language

  • Pinned notices for available bounties.
  • Ledger rows for current and completed work.
  • Reward seals labeled “XP,” never implying cash.
  • Serif headings paired with highly readable body text.

Voice & imagery

Warm, neighborly, and direct. Use “Claim bounty,” “Awaiting approval,” and “Not Yet” for real actions. Reserve flavor for headings such as “A helping hand is worth a story.” Prefer community, tools, gardens, and lantern imagery over combat or alcohol motifs.

Architecture responsibilities

Presentation

HTML/CSS, screens, navigation, forms, accessible feedback, responsive layouts, and Figma designs.

Business

Verification, claim eligibility, assignment, approvals, completion, point awards, communication permissions, and notification triggers.

Data

Users, bounties, claims, ratings, messages, notifications, and persistent status changes. Secure password storage belongs to the authentication implementation—not HTML.

Data the UI needs

User ID, name/username, photo and score; bounty ID, poster, description, materials, time, address, status and assigned hunter; claim ID, hunter and status; ratings tied to bounty and users; messages with sender, receiver and timestamp; notifications with type, related event and read state. Never expose password information to public UI.

08 / Team handoff

Build with the rules in view

Accessibility & reliability

  • Use semantic headings, visible field labels, keyboard navigation, and clear focus styles.
  • Keep touch targets approximately 44 pixels or larger.
  • Associate errors with fields; preserve input when submission fails.
  • Use status text alongside color. Announce asynchronous outcomes accessibly.
  • Prevent duplicate submissions and enforce transitions on the server.
  • Protect contact data and persist important changes.

Test checklist

  • Account validation and email verification.
  • Claim → accept and claim → reject branches.
  • Completion → Complete and completion → Not Yet branches.
  • No early or duplicate points; completed work leaves Current Bounty.
  • Private contact visible only to authorized users.
  • Denied notification permission and failed message delivery.
  • Empty lists, bad input, slow responses, and retry states.
  • Presentation → business → data → business → presentation integration.
  • Keyboard, phone width, desktop width, and text zoom.
